The Lich Ascendant is a mortal who has taken the first irreversible steps toward transcending death itself. Obsessed with mastering undeath and bending the boundaries of life, he views mortality as a prison from which only dark necromantic knowledge can offer escape. The Lich Ascendant’s relentless pursuit of immortality demands sacrifice—physical, spiritual, and moral—and few can walk this path without losing what makes them truly human. Yet to him, the shedding of mortal frailty is a small price to pay for dominion over death and eternity.

Unlike common necromancers who seek to command the undead as mere tools, the Lich Ascendant binds his very soul to a phylactery, anchoring his existence beyond the normal limits of life and death. His transformation is gradual, a creeping unlife that alters his body and mind alike—imbuing him with unnatural resilience, cold detachment, and a will so strong it defies the finality of death. To become a Lich Ascendant is to stand forever between the worlds of the living and the dead, an eternal sentinel of forbidden knowledge and unholy power.

**Role:** The Lich Ascendant is a master of necromantic arts, a dark figure whose power grows as he slips further from mortality. In adventuring parties, he offers devastating arcane might and uncanny survivability. His presence is often unsettling—half alive, half undead—and his ambitions frequently put him at odds with heroes and villains alike. His greatest foes are not other adventurers, but time and the divine forces that seek to end his unnatural existence.

**Alignment:** Most Lich Ascendants embrace evil or at least neutrality on the spectrum toward evil, their ambitions and means often too ruthless for true goodness. Some may cloak their nature with an outward veneer of lawful or neutral intentions, but beneath lies a cold, relentless hunger for immortality and power.

**Hit Die:** d6

# Requirements
To qualify to become a Lich Ascendant, a character must fulfill all the following criteria.

**Alignment:** Any evil.

**Skills:** Knowledge (Arcana) 5 ranks, Knowledge (Religion) 5 ranks.

**Feats:** Craft Wondrous Item, Spell Focus (Necromancy).

**Spells:** Must be able to cast 3rd-level arcane or divine spells. 

**Special:** The character must have successfully created or animated undead at least once.

# Class Features

## Deathless Doctrine (Ex)
At 1st level, a lich ascendant who possesses the Necromancy arcane school (or any of its subschools), the Death domain (or any of its subdomains), the undead bloodline, the Bones mystery, or the Bone spirit adds his lich ascendant levels to the effective levels of those classes when determining the effects and uses of associated school powers, domain abilities, bloodline powers, revelations, spirit abilities, and spirit hexes. This does not grant him any additional powers or abilities.

## Negative Energy Affinity (Ex)
At 1st level, a lich ascendant's soul begins to drift from the cycle of mortal life and death, drawing ever closer to the dark vitality of undeath.

He is healed by negative energy and harmed by positive energy as if he were undead. Spells and effects that deal negative energy (such as inflict light wounds) restore hit points to him, while those that channel positive energy (such as cure light wounds or channel positive energy) instead harm him.

## Hungerless Transition (Ex)
At 3rd level, the Lich Ascendant no longer needs to eat, drink, or breathe. His body has begun to reject mortal sustenance as it transitions toward undeath. This grants immunity to effects that require breathing, such as inhaled poisons and suffocation, and removes the need for food or water to survive. He is still subject to magical effects related to starvation, dehydration, or dehydration-based damage (such as horrid wilting).

Though he no longer sleeps in the conventional sense, the Lich Ascendant must enter a state of stillness and quiescence for 8 hours each day to maintain his physical and magical cohesion. This rest functions like rest for the purpose of regaining spell slots and preparing spells. While in this state, he is not unconscious, but is considered resting and vulnerable, and must remain mostly still.

## Shifting Psyche (Ex)
At 3rd level, a lich ascendant gains a +2 bonus on all saving throws against mind-affecting effects (including charms, compulsions, emotions, fear, and similar effects). This bonus increases to +4 at 7th level and to +6 at 11th level. At 15th level, he becomes immune to all mind-affecting effects.

## Foot in the Grave (Ex)
At 5th level, a lich ascendant’s connection to undeath deepens, and his body begins to shed the vulnerabilities of the living. He becomes immune to poison, disease, and sleep effects. Additionally, he gains resistance 10 to cold and electricity.

He also begins to draw vitality from his sheer force of will rather than his physical constitution. When calculating his hit points gained from class levels, he uses his Charisma modifier instead of his Constitution modifier. This benefit applies retroactively to all of his previous class levels.

## Phylactery (Su)
At 5th level, a lich ascendant completes a ritual to create a phylactery that anchors his soul, binding his existence beyond the normal limits of life and death. When he is reduced to 0 hit points or below, he immediately dies; he does not fall unconscious, stabilize, or remain in a dying state. His body is destroyed, and all worn or carried equipment—including his phylactery, if in his possession—is dropped where he fell.

His soul retreats into his phylactery, initiating a process of rejuvenation. After 1d10 days, his body reforms in the first unoccupied space nearest his phylactery, and he awakens fully healed. He can only rejuvenate in this way once per year. If he dies again before this year has passed, he must either wait the full year before rejuvenating again or be restored through magic such as raise dead or resurrection. Such spells restore him according to their normal effects and do not grant full healing as rejuvenation does.

His phylactery functions as a valid target for spells and effects that require a dead creature’s body for restoration, such as raise dead and resurrection, allowing these spells to function as if his body were present. However, it does not substitute for a physical corpse in cases where specific corpse interactions are required (e.g., restore corpse, gentle repose).

If his phylactery is destroyed, this bond is severed. Upon reaching 0 hit points thereafter, he dies instantly, his body is destroyed, and he cannot rejuvenate. He may only be restored by magic that does not require a corpse, such as true resurrection, a carefully worded wish followed by resurrection, or miracle.

He gains his phylactery outright at this level. Should his phylactery be lost or destroyed, he may craft and attune a new one using the Craft Wondrous Item feat. This crafting requires a period of downtime equal to the standard time for wondrous items and costs 5,000 gp per character level to complete. The ritual to attune to the new phylactery is complex and dangerous, requiring 24 hours of uninterrupted focus.

Regardless of its form, his phylactery has 40 hit points, hardness 20, and a break DC of 40. Its caster level matches his character level.

**Bonded Item:** If the lich ascendant possesses the arcane bond class feature (such as a wizard’s bonded object), he may choose to designate his phylactery as his bonded item. This allows him to harness the phylactery’s magical energy as his arcane bond, granting all associated benefits.

While bonded to his phylactery, he can use the bond to cast any one spell in his spellbook or known spells once per day, even if it is not prepared or has not been cast that day, as per the arcane bond ability. This ability functions regardless of whether he is in physical possession of the phylactery, and he does not suffer any concentration checks or penalties typically associated with casting spells without his bonded object worn or held.

If the phylactery is broken or destroyed, he loses the ability to use it as a bonded item until it is replaced and re-attuned.

## Deathless Resilience (Ex)
At 7th level, the Lich Ascendant's body becomes unnaturally resilient to pain and corruption. He gains DR 10/bludgeoning and magic. This damage reduction is doubled against nonlethal damage. At 11th level, this damage reduction increases by 2.

In addition, he becomes resistant to the effects of energy drain. He can still accrue negative levels, and may be killed if the total exceeds his Hit Dice, but he suffers no penalties from them. All negative levels he accrues are automatically removed after 24 hours without requiring a saving throw.

## Improved Phylactery (Su)
At 9th level, the Lich Ascendant enhances the power and resilience of his phylactery, deepening the bond between it and his soul. His rejuvenation process accelerates, and his ability to return from death becomes more reliable. He can now rejuvenate once per month instead of once per year.

His phylactery’s durability improves, increasing its hit points by 10, its hardness by 5, and its break DC by 2.

If he dies during this monthly cooldown, he must either wait the full month before rejuvenating again or be restored through magic such as raise dead or resurrection, which restore him according to their normal effects and do not grant full healing as rejuvenation does.

## One Foot Beyond (Ex)
At 9th level, a lich ascendant slips further from the frailties of mortal flesh. He becomes immune to paralysis, stunning, fatigue, and exhaustion. Additionally, his growing spiritual resilience allows him to use his Charisma modifier in place of his Constitution modifier when making Fortitude saving throws.

## Greater Phylactery (Su)
At 13th level, the Lich Ascendant's mastery over the necromantic arts further strengthens his phylactery, allowing him to rejuvenate repeatedly without limitation. He can now rejuvenate an unlimited number of times.

His phylactery’s durability increases again, gaining an additional 10 hit points, 5 hardness, and +2 to its break DC.

## Soul Beyond Reproach (Ex)
At 13th level, a lich ascendant’s soul transcends the reach of death itself. He becomes immune to death effects and energy drain (including both temporary and permanent negative levels). Effects that would bestow negative levels simply fail to affect him.

<h1 class="capstone-header">Capstone Abilities</h1>
At 20th character level, a character with sufficient investment in the Lich Ascendant prestige class may select the following capstone ability, in addition to any available universal capstone options.

## True Lich (Ex)
**Prerequisites:** Lich Ascendant 15.

The lich ascendant completes his apotheosis, fully transcending mortality. His type changes to undead, granting him all the traits of the undead type. He no longer has a Constitution score and becomes immune to any effect requiring a Fortitude save unless that effect also works on objects or is harmless. He gains channel resistance +4, gains immunity to Cold and Electric, and his damage reduction increases to 10/bludgeoning and magic.

Once per day, when the lich ascendant would be slain, his body collapses into a heap of ash and bone as though destroyed. At the beginning of his next turn, he reforms in the same square with full hit points, as if restored by true resurrection. This restoration occurs regardless of whether his phylactery remains intact, and ignores any existing limitations on the frequency or cooldown of his rejuvenation. This effect cannot be prevented, delayed, or diverted by any means short of divine intervention.
